# STUDY 3 — Human Models: Hypothetical Target Customers

Two phases, per the brief:

- **Phase A — Concentrated scope:** two composite personas drawn *only* from the five
  VECTOR-CORE traits where 3+ sources overlap. These two define the game's spine.
- **Phase B — Broadened lens:** seven edge-case personas that **each still had repeat data
  points**. These do not redefine the game; they add character and layers to it.

---

# PHASE A — THE CONCENTRATED CORE

## A1. "MULE" — Ray Delgado Boudreaux, 56
### *The solo owner-operator with his own authority*

> **One-line:** A man in the last decade of a 30-year career who owns the truck, the debt and the
> responsibility — and who is quietly terrified that the margin disappears before he can stop.

| Attribute | Spec | Evidence base |
|---|---|---|
| Age / generation | 56 · Gen X | ATRI: O/O avg 56; largest generation 40.8% |
| Sex | Male | ~92% of drivers |
| Ethnicity | White, non-Hispanic (with a Louisiana-adjacent mixed heritage) | 63% white among drivers; O/O skews whiter |
| Home | 22 acres outside a town of 4,100, between Memphis and Jackson — **Mississippi/Tennessee corridor** | BLS location quotients: MS 1.57, TN 1.49 |
| Marital / family | Married 27 years; one son in the Army, one daughter in community college | 72% of O/O married |
| Military | 6 years, Army motor transport (88M) | 26–36% of O/O are veterans |
| Education | HS diploma + a semester of community college | ~56% HS; ~14% college |
| Business | 2006 Kenworth T680, 812,000 miles, **own authority (MC number since 2016)** | 53% of carriers run exactly 1 truck |
| Equipment | Dry van, spot market; flirts with reefers in produce season | Dry van = largest class; reefer = higher rate + more detention |
| Miles / income | ~105,000 mi/yr · gross ~$232k · **net ~$68k** | ATBS: 94k mi avg, $64.5k net |
| Cost per mile | ~$1.92 all-in; freights at $1.88 | ATRI $2.26/mi (fleet, incl. driver wage); ATBS spot $1.88 |
| Faith / values | Southern Baptist, irregular attendance; blesses the truck's grille every morning out of habit, not theology | Regional/cultural; 60%+ married, rural-domiciled |
| Media diet | Classic country (Merle, Strait), sports talk, true-crime podcasts, YouTube dashcam channels, Fox/weather | Trucker-reported: music, podcasts, audiobooks, AM/FM |
| Extras | Black Lab "Diesel" rides along (~60% of drivers travel with a pet); a buck pole from last November; a Copenhagen habit he's "quitting" | TCA pet data |
| Phone | Samsung Galaxy A-series; cracked screen protector; 8 GB of a data plan he watches | ~63% Android; apps fail in dead zones |

**His day, in his own numbers:**
- 04:40 — wakes in the bunk, checks the board before his boots are on.
- 05:10–16:10 — 11 hours of driving against a 14-hour clock, with 2–4 hours of it *not* driving.
- Dock and receiver waits: **3.5 hrs average, 14 hrs/week** — this is his **principal entertainment window**.
- 17:00–18:30 — shower, food, phone: **his single longest uninterrupted "leisure" block of the day.**
- 21:00 — lights out in the sleeper. **10 hours off; roughly 90 minutes of that is spent awake on the phone.**

**What he wants but cannot have:**
- To be *paid for his time* (he has collected detention fees on fewer than a third of the loads he billed).
- To stop being invisible ("I'm a $250,000 business and I'm treated like a steering wheel holder").
- Margin he can defend. **The fantasy is not more driving — it is margin without the grind.**

**Why he'll play:**
1. He has 3–5 hours a day of interruptible dead time that nothing respects.
2. He already plays idle tycoons and 8-ball; he self-reports playing Balatro at docks.
3. He wants a version of his own life where the numbers *work*, and he wants it to be honest.
4. He wants a place where "owner-operator" means *boss*.

**How he'll punish you:**
- One forced ad on the settlement screen and he's gone, and he'll tell 400 people in a Facebook group.
- One condescending tutorial or a mascot that winks and he'll call it a kids' game.
- One 40-minute session requirement and it's deleted by Tuesday.

## A2. "TRIANGLE" — Denise "Two-Bar" Whitaker, 47
### *The micro-fleet manager who still drives*

> **One-line:** Runs three trucks, a kitchen-table dispatch office and her own shift behind the
> wheel — the operational brain of a 3–10 truck carrier, which is 22% of all U.S. carriers.

| Attribute | Spec | Evidence base |
|---|---|---|
| Age / sex | 47 · female — a meaningful and under-served slice of the **3–10 truck band** | Women are 12.6% of all truck-transport jobs though only ~4–6% of long-haul Class A; the micro-fleet/admin layer is where women actually are |
| Home | Rural Kentucky/Ohio corridor, 3 trucks, 2 leased-on drivers + herself | 45–58% of drivers are rural-domiciled |
| Business | 3 power units; dry van + one reefer; factoring; CSA-score-obsessed; insurance renewal is her personal Super Bowl | FMCSA: 22% of carriers run 3–10 trucks |
| Marital | Married to a driver who now runs local; two teenagers still home | 40% of female drivers have kids under 18 at home |
| Education | Associate degree in business (she is the credentialed exception) | ~14% of drivers hold a degree, skewing to small-business owners |
| Play style | Puzzles and word games; plays to *decompress*, not to compete; hates pressure timers | Older-adult design research: solo, low-pressure, "brain training" motives |
| Stressors | Driver no-shows, a $2.40/mile van contract she bid too low, ELD audit, a broker who won't pay | Detention + rate environment data |
| Phone | iPhone 12 hand-me-down from her daughter | 37% of truckers use iOS |

**Why she matters to the design:**
She is the **proof that the game must span "driver" and "operator."** If the only fantasy is driving,
you lose her. If the only fantasy is spreadsheets, you lose Ray. The game's progression spine
(1 truck → 3 trucks → 10 trucks) is specifically designed so both personas play the same game on
the same day and simply occupy different points on the arc.

She is also the audience for the game's **respectful-representation standard**: no pin-up mudflaps
in the art, no waitress-mascot, no "trucker wife" jokes. Ray is fine with that too — it's his
wife and daughter.

# PHASE B — BROADENED LENS: THE EDGE CASES (each with repeat data)

These are people who sit *outside* the concentrated core but whose traits recur across multiple
independent datasets. Each becomes a **layer** in the design, not a separate product.

---

## B1. "REEFER QUEEN" — Renata, 44 · Female long-haul owner-operator
- **Repeat data:** women = 4.1% of Class A CDL holders but 9.5% of all professional drivers;
  median age of female truckers 46; **65% of female drivers are married** (higher than men);
  58% rural-domiciled; 40% have kids under 18 at home; women's stops include detention **49% of the time**
  (vs 39% overall); more than a third of women's stops involve short waits (vs 27% for men);
  women's median heavy-truck wage $52,800; **owner-operator women average ~$180,000 gross**;
  30% of female truckers are independent owner-operators.
- **Design layer:** the **Home-Time Promise** mechanic (you commit to a date and the game scores you
  on it, with real in-game consequences either way), **safety-weighted routing**, a
  **"Sisters of the Road"** convoy chapter, and physical-safety as a route attribute — because for
  a woman parking overnight is a routing *risk* decision, not a convenience one.
- **Founding hire #1: "Renata" — Reefer Queen.** Unlocked in Produce Season. Her questline is
  about earning authority in a room full of men without becoming one of them.

## B2. "EL PATRÓN" — Hector, 41 · Hispanic owner-operator, family carrier
- **Repeat data:** Hispanic drivers rose from 19% → **23%**, with a sharp inflection in 2021;
  Mexican-origin = 56.1% of Hispanic drivers; Hispanic drivers are also younger and are
  disproportionately represented in small fleets; **Laredo, TX** is the #1 U.S. truck border port
  (~$20B).
- **Design layer:** **Spanish as a first-class language, not a settings toggle**; bilingual radio
  hosts (the same host, two feeds — this is how real Spanish-language radio in the U.S. works);
  **"Family Authority"** mechanic where relatives are hires with loyalty bonuses and family
  drama questlines; **cross-border lanes** (Laredo / El Paso / Otay Mesa) as a late-game vertical
  with customs, bond and broker mechanics.
- **Founding hire #2: "Hector" — El Patrón.** His questline: buying his brother's truck off him
  and keeping the family name on the door.

## B3. "SIX" — Marcellus, 38 · Veteran driver
- **Repeat data:** **26% of owner-operators have served in the military (OOIDA); other sources put
  owner-operators at 34% and all truckers at ~10% (2× the national rate)**; veterans are
  over-represented in trucking even after controlling for age and sex (Census).
- **Design layer:** **"Convoy Ops"** — a formal, procedurally rigid mode with mission briefings,
  timed but *generous* stages, radio discipline, and a **commendation/ribbon collection** that
  feeds the persistent Legacy score. Also: impeccable reliability as a *feature*.
- **Founding hire #3: "Six" — the veteran** who cannot sleep in a house yet and can plan a
  700-mile run in his head.

## B4. "DEEJ" — Deja, 24 · Gen Z rookie
- **Repeat data:** Gen Z = 7.5% of drivers; only **20% of drivers are under 35** vs 35% of the
  labor force; only 6% of U.S. drivers are under 25; average male entry age 26.
  **The industry has a documented succession crisis.** ATRI explicitly frames recruiting younger,
  more diverse entrants as the sector's central challenge.
- **Design layer:** **"Apprentice" mode** (start as a company driver, earn your truck);
  **Mentorship** — players earn Reputation for training rookies, which makes the veteran player the
  *hero* of the new generation's story rather than a gatekeeper. Vertical shareable clips.
- **Founding hire #4: "Deej" — the rookie.** Her questline is the succession story: who gets the
  keys when Ray hangs them up.

## B5. "HOSS" — Waylon, 68 · Near-retirement owner-operator
- **Repeat data:** planned retirement age **70**; average owner-operator age 56 and rising; the
  veteran cohort (Gen X + Boomers) is **62% of the workforce** and is approaching a mass exit;
  owner-operators average 20+ years in the seat.
- **Design layer:** the **"Hang Up the Keys"** endgame — sell the authority, sell the truck, lease
  it out with your name on it, or hand it to family. A **Legacy Score** persists across playthroughs
  as the "name on the door." This is the single deepest emotional vein available to the design:
  an audience of men in the last decade of their careers, being asked how they want to be remembered.
- **Founding hire #5: "Hoss" — the old-timer.** He's your mentor's old running buddy, and he's the
  one who tells the player the legendary road stories on the radio.

## B6. The Specialty Haulers (an equipment vertical, not a person)
- **Repeat data (FMCSA cargo classes × small-fleet share):** livestock 93.2% small fleet;
  ag/grain 91.6%; auto transport 89.6%; log/lumber 89.3%; household goods 84.4%; reefer 83.9%;
  intermodal 83.7%; tanker 69.4%; chemical 69.1% (with 51–54% hazmat overlap).
  Detention by sector: **reefer 56.2% of stops**, dry van 32.6%, flatbed 26.2%, specialized 19.3%.
  Rates: dry van $1.80–2.40 · reefer $2.20–3.00 · flatbed $2.10–2.80 · tanker $2.75 · hazmat $3.25.
- **Design layer:** each vertical is a **content season** with its own mechanics —
  **flatbed** = tarping/securement labour + oversize permits + wind-load risk;
  **reefer** = temperature logging, produce-season rate spikes, worst detention;
  **tanker/hazmat** = endorsement gates, higher rates, safety-score pressure;
  **auto transport** = damage-risk grading; **livestock** = live-load urgency timers;
  **logs** = weight and seasonal access rules.
- **This alone is 18+ months of live-ops content from public data.**

## B7. The Team/Family & Pet Layer
- **Repeat data:** **~60% of drivers travel with a pet** (TCA); husband-and-wife teams are an
  established segment; 72–93% of carriers are 1–2 truck operations where the spouse frequently
  dispatches; female drivers are 65% married vs. 55% of men.
- **Design layer:** **Two-Shift Team mode** — one driver works while you're offline, the other is
  active when you are (a beautiful idle mechanic: the truck never technically stops);
  **Spouse Dispatcher** hire with an efficiency bonus; **a dog companion ("Axle")** with a morale
  buff, a customisation slot, and a quest chain; family cabin customisation.
  This is the single highest-affection-per-line-of-code feature in the plan.

## B8. The Regional Map Layer
- **Repeat data:** MS 1.57 / TN 1.49 / DE 1.41 / TX 1.12 / LA 1.11 location quotients; top metros
  Houston, Chicago, DFW, Atlanta, Memphis; Laredo = #1 border port; Lakeland→Miami = busiest
  intrastate flatbed lane.
- **Design layer:** a **stylised, real-corridor U.S. map** (I-10, I-40, I-80, I-95), real freight
  markets as economic modifiers, produce season, construction season, harvest season, winter chain
  law zones, and named "black hole" receivers — **parodied**, not licensed.
- **Legal note:** parody shipper names (*Vall-Mart, America-Cold, Kro-Grr, Titan Foods*) avoid
  trademark exposure while being instantly legible to the audience.

## How the two phases fit together

```
PHASE A  →  the spine      Ray + Denise     →  core loop, session shape, monetisation ethics,
                                               90% of the design decisions

PHASE B  →  the flesh      Renata, Hector, Six, Deej, Hoss,
                           the specialty verticals, teams+pets, the map
                           →  expansion modules, seasons, questlines, endgame, longevity
```

Every Phase-B layer is a *drop*, not a second app. That is deliberate: a narrow, identity-tight
audience of a few million gets more value from one deep, socially-shared game than from three
shallow ones competing for the same phone.
