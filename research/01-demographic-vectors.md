# STUDY 1 — Demographic Vectors: U.S. Single Owner-Operators & Micro-Fleet Managers

**Scope:** Solo owner-operators (1 truck, own authority or leased-on) and micro-fleet operators
(2–10 power units) in the United States. This is a subset of the ~3.5M U.S. truck drivers, and it
is the *most* demographically distinct slice of it.

**Research question:** What are the most common demographics, and which overlapping clusters are
strong enough to design entertainment products against?

---

## 0. Why this slice is worth isolating

| Structural fact | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| U.S. motor carriers operating **10 trucks or fewer** | **91.5%** | FMCSA census via ATA *American Trucking Trends 2025* |
| Carriers operating **exactly 1 truck** | **~53%** | FMCSA MCMIS |
| Carriers operating 2 trucks | ~16% | FMCSA |
| Carriers operating 3–10 trucks | ~22% | FMCSA |
| Carriers under 100 power units | 99.3% | FMCSA / ATA |
| Active for-hire interstate carriers | ~580,000 | ATA 2025 |
| Owner-operators (industry count) | ~400,000–580,000 | Industry estimates |
| New interstate authority applicants running 1–5 trucks (2024, all quarters) | **98%** | FMCSA via CarrierDetails |
| Average annual miles, owner-operator | **94,000** (2024), up from 91,400 in 2023 | ATBS |

**Read:** American trucking is not a business of giants. It is ~1.3M micro-businesses. The
"single owner-operator / micro-fleet manager" is not a niche — it *is* the industry, and it is
the most self-identified, culturally coherent occupational group in the country.

## 1. The Hard Demographics

### 1.1 Sex — the single strongest vector

| Metric | Range across sources | Best estimate |
|---|---|---|
| Truck drivers, men | 86% – 96.1% | **~92%** |
| Women, all professional drivers | 4.1% (ATRI 2024, Class A CDL) – 9.5% (Women in Trucking) | **~6–9.5%** |
| Women, owner-operator-specific | 6% – 10.6% | **~8–10%** |
| Women in all truck-transportation jobs (incl. dispatch, admin, CS) | 12.6% | ATRI 2025 |

> **Confidence: VERY HIGH. ~9 in 10 owner-operators and micro-fleet principals are men.**
> Note the *micro-fleet* band is slightly less male-skewed than the solo-driver band: a meaningful
> share of 3–10 truck operations are run by women (dispatch/admin/ownership roles), which matters
> later as an edge case.

### 1.2 Age — the second strongest vector

| Metric | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Average age, all U.S. truck drivers (govt data) | **47** (vs. 42 for total U.S. workforce) | BLS / ATRI |
| Average age, **owner-operators** (industry data, 2024) | **56** — was 50 two decades ago | ATRI 2025 |
| OOIDA member modal band | **51–60** represents 59% of respondents | OOIDA Foundation 2024 survey |
| Average age, owner-operators (alternate estimates) | 46 – 59 | Zippia / industry surveys |
| Average entry age into trucking | Male **26** / Female **40** | ATRI |

**Generation mix of the truck driver workforce:**

| Generation | Share |
|---|---|
| Gen X (1965–1980) | **40.8%** ← largest |
| Millennials | 30.7% |
| Baby Boomers | 20.7% |
| Gen Z | 7.5% |
| Under 35 | ~20% (vs. 35% of the overall labor force) |

> **Confidence: VERY HIGH** that the modal owner-operator is **Gen X, born roughly 1966–1974,
> aged 50–58**, who entered the trade in his mid-20s and has 25+ years of seat time.
> The "47 average" figure is the whole-driver population; the owner-operator population runs **8–10
> years older**. Both numbers are true; they describe different people.

### 1.3 Race & Ethnicity — the fastest-moving vector

| Group | Share of truck drivers |
|---|---|
| White | **63%** (was 77% a decade ago) |
| Black / African American | **23%** (was 15% a decade ago) |
| Hispanic / Latino | **23%** (was 19% a decade ago) |
| Asian | ~4% |
| Non-White, non-Hispanic | ~25.9% and rising |
| Owner-operators specifically | White ~61.7%, Hispanic 17.1%, Black 12.1% (Zippia/Census) |
| OOIDA membership specifically | Predominantly Caucasian (association skews older/whiter than the workforce) |

> **Confidence: HIGH, but the "owner-operator" and "association member" cuts skew whiter and older
> than the overall driver workforce.** Design implication: the *core* is white Gen X male, but the
> *growth* is Black and Hispanic, and it inflected sharply in 2021.

### 1.4 Marital, family & veteran status — the loyalty vectors

| Metric | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Owner-operators married | **72%** | OOIDA Foundation 2024 |
| Male truckers married | >60% (vs. 40% of women) | ATRI |
| Owner-operators who served in the military | **26%–36%** | OOIDA Foundation 2024 |
| All truckers who are veterans | ~10% — **double the rate of the general workforce** | U.S. Census Bureau |
| Households with kids under 18 at home (female drivers) | 40% | Industry survey data |
| Drivers who travel with a pet | **~60%** | Truckload Carriers Association |
| Planned retirement age | **70** | OOIDA Foundation 2024 |
| Nights per year away from home (>151) | 60%+ of owner-operators | Industry data |

> **Confidence: HIGH.** Veteran status is one of the strongest *and most under-used* targeting
> vectors in this audience: **1 in 3 owner-operators is a veteran**, versus 1 in 10 workers
> generally.

### 1.5 Education — the "plain-language" vector

| Highest attainment | Share of drivers |
|---|---|
| High school diploma / GED | ~56% |
| Less than high school | 12.7–16% (down from 20%) |
| Some college / associate / vocational | ~25% |
| **College degree or higher** | **~14%** (up from 9% in 2008; U.S. workforce average is ~50%) |

> **Confidence: HIGH.** The owner-operator is not a low-information consumer — he is an
> *uncredentialed expert*. He runs P&L, IFTA, CSA scores and insurance negotiations. He is
> numerate and operationally sophisticated but **allergic to jargon, academic framing and
> being talked down to**.

### 1.6 Geography & income

| Metric | Figure |
|---|---|
| Highest location quotient (heavy/tractor-trailer drivers) | **Mississippi 1.57, Tennessee 1.49, Delaware 1.41, Louisiana 1.11, Texas 1.12** |
| Largest absolute counts | Texas (~210k), California, Pennsylvania, Florida, Ohio |
| Top freight metros | Houston, Chicago, DFW, Atlanta, Memphis |
| Rural residence | 45% of male drivers, 58% of female drivers (industry survey data) |
| Favorite domicile states (no income tax) | **Texas, Florida, Tennessee** |
| Owner-operator net income (ATBS clients 2024–25) | **$64,524** (+2.5% YoY); 2025 estimate **~$71,800** |
| Typical net range | $60,000–$120,000 |
| Gross revenue | $200,000–$350,000 |
| Average cost to operate | **$2.26/mile** total (ATRI 2025: fuel $0.48, truck payment $0.39, maintenance $0.198, insurance $0.102) |
| Spot rate environment (mid-2025) | **$1.88/mile** — flat vs. 3.5 years prior; load-to-truck ratio ~88:1 (was 240:1 in Nov 2021) |
| Market churn | ~88,000 authorities revoked in 2023; ~10,000 net carriers lost in H1 2024 |

> **Confidence: HIGH.** Southern + Midwestern, rural-adjacent, freight-corridor-domiciled,
> middle-income, high-gross/low-margin, with genuinely brutal cash-flow anxiety. He is
> simultaneously a "six-figure business" and a man checking his factoring account at 4 a.m.

## 2. The Behavioral Vectors (what actually drives them)

These matter more than the census columns, because they're what the product is built against.

| # | Vector | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | **Autonomy is the #1 motive** | ATRI: the top reasons drivers become owner-operators are *Independence / ability to set hours*, *Schedule flexibility*, *Choice of routes* — ranked **above** healthcare and retirement savings (considered important by fewer than 40%) | ★★★★★ |
| B2 | **Enormous, predictable dead time** | Average dock wait **3.5 hrs**; detention occurs on **39.3% of all stops**; **117–209 hours/yr** detained per driver; OOIDA members report **14 hrs/week** in detention; 61.6% of trucks idle >3 hrs while parked | ★★★★★ |
| B3 | **Isolation & loneliness** | **27.9%** report loneliness, **26.9%** depression, **20.6%** chronic sleep problems, **14.5%** anxiety; avg **4.78 hrs** sleep/24hr; truckers are 4th–5th highest occupational suicide risk | ★★★★★ |
| B4 | **A rigid daily rhythm** | 11 hrs drive / 10 hrs off / 14-hr on-duty window; long-haul drivers spend 5–21+ days per trip (26% report 22+ day trips) | ★★★★★ |
| B5 | **Phone-first, Android-leaning, data-poor** | 87% of drivers use smartphones daily at work, ~37% of work-app installs are iOS (**i.e. ~63% Android**); 33% use apps for weather/fuel/traffic; 20% no longer carry laptops; apps break because *"the lack of internet access in remote areas"*; biggest frustration is small screens (5.5" ideal) | ★★★★☆ |
| B6 | **Tech must be a tool, not a toy** | Driver complaints: apps freezing, loading slowly, crashing, draining battery, **forced password changes and 2FA**; 87% prefer employers who invest in tech; 58% cite small-screen friction | ★★★★☆ |
| B7 | **Ad-hostile, not pay-hostile** | Actual review on a top truck-sim competitor: *"you have to watch ads just to pause the game… a monthly charge as expensive as the real life load board"* | ★★★★★ |
| B8 | **Status & respect hunger** | Owner-operators are economically invisible and institutionally unheard; "freedom and independence" cited by 30% as what they most enjoy; the cultural self-image is *King of the Road*, not *steering-wheel holder* | ★★★★☆ |
| B9 | **Craft & machine pride** | Deep emotional investment in the rig itself: chrome, lights, paint, stacks, CB gear, dash trinkets; rig customisation is the primary visible status marker of the trade | ★★★★☆ |
| B10 | **Storyteller culture** | CB radio folklore, war stories, tall tales, handles, "Convoy" / *Smokey and the Bandit* / *Ice Road Truckers* mythology; trucking is one of the last oral-culture trades | ★★★★☆ |
| B11 | **Institutional distrust** | Brokers, mega-carriers, insurers, FMCSA, ELD mandates; only **29.3%** of carriers collect the detention fees they bill; **44%** of facilities won't let a driver use a bathroom | ★★★★☆ |
| B12 | **Age-appropriate competition profile** | Quantic Foundry: **competition appeal declines most with age; strategy is the most age-stable motivation**; older adults rank *challenge* as their #1 play motive and prefer playing solo over social obligation | ★★★★★ |

## 3. Clip: the five strongest overlapping vectors

These are the intersections where **three or more independent sources agree**. They are the
"VECTOR-CORE" of this audience — the load-bearing traits a product must satisfy.

```
V1  MALE, GEN X, 50–58, MARRIED, VETERAN-HOUSEHOLD, SOUTH/MIDWEST, RURAL-ADJACENT
    → 9/10 male · modal 51–60 · 72% married · 26–36% veteran · loc-quotient MS/TN/TX
    → a man in the last decade of his career with a spouse and a mortgage and a truck note

V2  TIME-RICH, ATTENTION-POOR, DEVICE-CONSTRAINED
    → 3.5 hr waits · 14 hr/wk detention · 10 hr break · Android · patchy signal
    → "I have 60 minutes but I can't give you 60 seconds of undivided attention"

V3  STRESSED, ISOLATED, RESPONSIBILITY-LOADED, SELF-RELIANT
    → 28% lonely · 27% depressed · $2.26/mi cost vs $1.88/mi rate · 4.78 hr sleep
    → the fantasy must be relief and control, not more pressure

V4  PROUD, INDEPENDENT, STATUS-SEEKING, INSTITUTIONALLY DISTRUSTFUL
    → independence > benefits · 29.3% collect their detention fees · anti-broker sentiment
    → respect is the currency; pity is poison

V5  NUMERATE, UNCREDENTIALED EXPERT, ALLERGIC TO BEING SOLD TO
    → 14% college but full P&L fluency · reviews torch greedy monetisation
    → comprehension is easy; condescension and dark patterns are fatal
```

## 4. Data conflicts — read this before quoting numbers anywhere

| Claim | Why sources disagree | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Average owner-operator age = 46 | Zippia/careers sites blend all "owner/operator" roles incl. non-trucking business owners | Don't use 46 for trucking. Use **51–58**. |
| Average owner-operator age = 56 | ATRI 2024 industry data, owner-operator self-report | Primary figure |
| Women = 4% | ATRI, Class A CDL holders only | Use for "long-haul Class A" |
| Women = 9.5% | Women in Trucking, all professional drivers | Use for "professional drivers" |
| Women = 33.2% owner/operator | Zippia — includes non-trucking "owner/operator" business roles | Discard |
| White = 72% vs 63% | Older dataset vs. ATRI 2024 10-year trend | Use 63% + trend line |
| Veterans = 10% vs 34% | All truckers vs. owner-operators / OOIDA members | Use both, labelled |
