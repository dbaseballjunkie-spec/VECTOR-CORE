# BROADENING THE LENS — Edge-Case Layers & the One-vs-Many Decision

Two things happen in this document:

1. **The broadened lens:** the seven edge-case cohorts from Study 3 become *content layers* inside
   the flagship, each tied to a repeat data point.
2. **The portfolio decision:** one game with depth vs. several games — with the answer, the
   reasoning, and the one satellite product genuinely worth building.

---

## PART 1 — EDGE-CASE LAYERS

Rule: an edge case earns its way into the game **only if the trait repeats across independent
datasets**. Every layer below has that. Each is a *content drop* into the same app — never a fork.

### Layer 1 — "HOME TIME" (the female-driver layer)
**Data anchor:** women are 4.1% of Class A CDL holders but 9.5% of all professional drivers;
median age 46; 65% married; 58% rural; 40% with children under 18 at home; 30% are independent
owner-operators; women's stops involve detention 49% of the time vs 39% overall.
**Layer contents:**
- **The Home-Time Promise** — you commit to a date at home, and the game scores you against it.
  Breaking it costs Reputation *and* a visible family-morale stat. Keeping it pays a multiplier
  ("a promise kept is a reputation earned").
- **Safety-weighted routing** — parking, lighting, and facility safety become *route attributes*,
  not set dressing. Choosing a well-lit yard costs fuel; the game never lectures, it prices.
- **"Sisters of the Road"** — a convoy chapter and a mentorship network with the cohort's own
  leaderboard.
- **Representation standard** — no pin-up art, no mascot, no "trucker wife" jokes. The default
  player character is a 56-year-old man, and the art never treats anyone as decoration.

### Layer 2 — "LA FAMILIA" (the Hispanic / immigrant-owner layer)
**Data anchor:** Hispanic drivers 19% → **23%** in a decade with a sharp 2021 inflection; 56.1% of
Hispanic drivers are of Mexican origin; **Laredo is the #1 U.S. truck border port** (~$20B).
**Layer contents:**
- **Spanish as a first-class language**: not a settings toggle — a fully authored parallel script,
  including the radio show. Same host, two feeds, exactly like real U.S. Spanish-language radio.
- **"Family Authority"** — hire relatives; family hires carry loyalty bonuses and unlock a family
  drama questline (the brother who wants to buy in; the nephew who doesn't want to drive).
- **Cross-border vertical (late game):** Laredo / El Paso / Otay Mesa lanes with customs,
  bonds, broker relationships and a genuine speed-vs-risk trade-off.

### Layer 3 — "CONVOY OPS" (the veteran layer)
**Data anchor:** **26% of owner-operators have served (OOIDA)**; other sources put owner-operators
at 34% and all truckers at ~10% — **double the national rate**, and trucking remains a destination
occupation for veterans even after controlling for age and sex.
**Layer contents:**
- A procedurally structured mode: mission briefings, staged routes, radio discipline, a
  **commendation and ribbon collection** feeding the persistent Legacy score.
- Explicitly generous timers, crystal-clear objectives, no ambiguity — the mode is designed for
  people who find procedure calming rather than constraining.
- A **"Battle Buddy"** pairing system: two players run the same corridor as a team for a week.

### Layer 4 — "THE APPRENTICE" (the Gen Z / succession layer)
**Data anchor:** Gen Z = 7.5% of drivers; only **20% of drivers are under 35** vs 35% of the
workforce; 6% under 25; average male entry age 26. **ATRI names this a workforce crisis.**
**Layer contents:**
- **Apprentice mode:** start as a company driver with no truck, earn the down payment, buy the rig.
  It is a whole alternate on-ramp to the same game — and it is a *recruitment fantasy* aimed at the
  audience the industry desperately needs.
- **Mentorship pays.** Veterans earn Reputation for training rookies; the game makes the older
  player the hero of the younger player's story rather than an obstacle to it.
- **Shareable vertical clips** of settlement cards, handshake wins and truck builds — the native
  acquisition channel for the younger cohort.

### Layer 5 — "HANG UP THE KEYS" (the near-retirement / legacy layer)
**Data anchor:** planned retirement age **70**; owner-operator average age **56** and rising;
Gen X + Boomers are **62% of the workforce** and are approaching a mass exit; O/O average
20+ years in the seat.
**Layer contents:**
- The **endgame choice**: sell to Meridian · lease your truck out with your name on it · franchise ·
  hand the keys to a rookie like Deej · or keep running until the wheels come off.
- **Legacy Score** persists across every prestige reset — the name on the door gets longer, and
  the terminal wall fills with your history.
- A dignified, written, *earned* ending. This is the emotional pinnacle of the entire product and
  it exists nowhere else in the market.

### Layer 6 — "THE VERTICALS" (the specialty-haul layer)
**Data anchor (FMCSA cargo census × small-fleet share):** livestock 93.2% small fleet · ag/grain
91.6% · auto transport 89.6% · log/lumber 89.3% · household goods 84.4% · reefer 83.9% ·
intermodal 83.7% · tanker 69.4% · chemical 69.1% (51–54% with hazmat). Detention: reefer 56.2%
of stops, dry van 32.6%, flatbed 26.2%, specialized 19.3%.
**Layer contents — one drop per vertical, each with genuinely different mechanics:**

| Vertical | New mechanic | Real hook |
|---|---|---|
| **Flatbed** | Tarping & securement labour mini-decisions; oversize permits; wind load | Higher pay, more work, more risk |
| **Reefer** | Temperature logging; a load that can *spoil*; produce-season rate spikes | Worst detention in the industry; best seasonal money |
| **Tanker / Hazmat** | Endorsement gates; safety-score pressure; route restrictions | $2.75–$3.25/mile |
| **Auto transport** | Damage grading; load/unload sequencing | 89.6% small-fleet dominated |
| **Livestock** | Live-load urgency; heat and cold stress; rest-stop rules | 93.2% small-fleet — the purest owner-operator niche |
| **Logs / Grain** | Weight limits; seasonal access; mill schedules | 89–92% small-fleet |

→ **This is 18+ months of live-ops content from public data, with no invention required.**

### Layer 7 — "SECOND SEAT" (the team, family & pet layer)
**Data anchor:** **~60% of drivers travel with a pet**; husband-and-wife teams are an established
segment; 72–93% of carriers are 1–2 trucks where the spouse frequently dispatches; female drivers
are 65% married vs 55% of men.
**Layer contents:**
- **Two-Shift Team mode** — while you're offline, your partner drives. *The truck never stops.*
  It is the best idle mechanic in the design and it falls straight out of the data.
- **Spouse Dispatcher** hire — settlement efficiency bonus; a shared-account terminal.
- **"Axle"** — a dog companion with a morale buff, a customisation slot (a bandana, a bed, a
  window perch) and a quest chain. Highest affection-per-line-of-code in the project.
- **The family cabin** — photos, a kid's drawing taped to the dash, a laminated school picture.

## PART 2 — THE ONE-VS-MANY DECISION

### The brief's question: *"Either design one game that way with depth or design a few different
games. Whichever would get played more."*

### THE ANSWER: **ONE GAME, WITH DEPTH.**

### Why — in order of weight

**1. The audience is narrow and identity-tight. Networks, not catalogues, win narrow audiences.**
The total addressable core is ~3.5M U.S. drivers plus ~1.3M micro-carrier businesses and their
families — call it 6–8M Americans, of whom perhaps 1.5–2.5M are realistic players. Three games
split that base into thirds and none of them reaches the critical mass for a convoy system, a
weekly settlement ritual, or word-of-mouth.
**The single game's social layer is the retention product.** Fragmentation kills it.

**2. The word-of-mouth channel is a single pipe, and it holds one message.**
Trucker Facebook groups, r/Truckers, trucking YouTube, OOIDA-adjacent media, truck-stop Geofence
ads — these are *concentrated, high-trust, single-message* channels. "Everybody's playing
Big Rig Tycoon" is a message. "There are three of these games and they're all fine" is not.

**3. The genre already has the depth mechanism built in.** An idle tycoon with a card layer
scales by *adding verticals, seasons and hires* — not by shipping new apps. The 18 months of
content in Part 1 costs a fraction of a second product and carries the same retention value.

**4. The edge cases from Phase B aren't separate markets — they're the same man's life.**
Ray has a wife who dispatches, a daughter entering the industry, a veteran buddy, a dog, and a
retirement date. Splitting those into three apps would be *anthropologically wrong*. They belong
in one game, in layers, the way they belong in one life.

**5. Production and marketing economics.** One live-ops team, one seasonal calendar, one app-store
presence, one brand, one community. Two of three games would die of neglect — and in a trust-driven
niche, an abandoned game is worse than no game.

### When you SHOULD build the second product

Not instead of the flagship — **in front of it**, and not really as a game at all.

## THE ONE SATELLITE WORTH BUILDING: **"LOAD BOARD" — the daily puzzle**

> **A 60-second daily puzzle. One load. One optimal answer. A shareable result. Free. No ads.**

**Format (Wordle's shape, trucking's content):**
Every day, one load card is published. Players choose: **accept · decline · counter.**
Then they set the route: fuel stop, break, weigh station.
The optimal line depends on rate/mile, deadhead, weight, weather and the clock — all deterministic
and all derived from the same economy engine as the flagship.
Results are shared as a compact grid:

```
 B I G  R I G   #114
 🚚✅ $2.31/mi  •  Deadhead 12 mi  •  Clock +0:40
 🟩🟩🟨⬜⬜
 3/5 loads won this week
```

**Why this is the right second product:**
- **It costs almost nothing** — it reuses the flagship's economy model and its card art.
- **It is the single best user-acquisition engine available to this brand.** Wordle's mechanic is
  shareability inside a tight, talkative community; trucker Facebook groups are *exactly* that
  community, and they have a demonstrated appetite for arguing about rate per mile.
- **It is not a competing game** — it's a daily habit that funnels into the flagship ("want the
  full board? → Big Rig Tycoon").
- **It generates the cleanest possible research dataset:** millions of real decisions about real
  load economics, which then tunes the flagship's difficulty curve.
- **Zero twitch, zero cost, zero ad load, 45 seconds.** It is the most respectful product you can
  put in front of this audience.

**Portfolio verdict:**
```
 SHIP:   Big Rig Tycoon (flagship, deep, live-ops, one game)
 SHIP:   LOAD BOARD (free daily puzzle = the acquisition funnel + tuneable data engine)
 NEVER:  three mid-sized truck games competing for the same thumb
```
